01 — A working example

One food distributor.
Six apps in production.
One engineer.

B&R Food Services delivers to restaurants across Los Angeles every day. Since 2025 I've been the only engineer behind everything their office, drivers and admins use. Scroll through the estate.

Step 1 · The platform

Drivers stopped carrying paper.

A role-based admin console and a driver portal: routes, deliveries, digitized data capture, real-time updates over Socket.IO, and payment capture with a QuickBooks audit trail. 199 commits, used every working day.

Step 2 · The people

Clock-ins that know where you are.

WorkSync handles attendance, breaks, leave and shift scheduling for the whole team, with location-verified clock-ins and break-compliance rules. I also fixed the overlap validation that had been quietly letting conflicting shifts through.

Django RESTReact 18CeleryRedis

Step 3 · The night

The night-shift report writes itself.

Nightshift turns the evening's paperwork into a clean report the morning team can act on, behind proper authentication after the server was rebuilt clean. Small app, big habit change.

FastAPIVitenginxsystemd

Step 4 · The reconciliation

A scanned PDF in, a 12-tab workbook out.

The daily route report reads a scanned route PDF with vision models and produces a fully reconciled Excel workbook — twelve tabs, every delivery and dollar accounted for — instead of an afternoon of retyping.

FastAPIClaude visionopenpyxlReact + Vite

Step 5 · The suppliers

Weekly price updates, no human in the loop.

Suppliers email price lists in every format imaginable. The pipeline reads forwarded threads, attachments and headerless forwards, works out which vendor each belongs to, and reconciles the deltas into inventory workbooks. 92 test files built from real malformed inputs.

FastAPIOCRClaude + GeminiEmail ingestion

Step 6 · The documents

Thirty PDFs at once, text or scanned.

A Flask service that pulls product IDs, descriptions and costs out of vendor price lists — even image-only tables — re-architected as a streaming page-by-page pipeline so big multi-vendor documents stop blowing up memory.

FlaskTesseractPopplerStreaming

Underneath · The platform

Then I made it boring to run.

One identity realm (Keycloak SSO) instead of six logins, secrets in Infisical instead of hand-carried .env files, GitHub Actions gating every deploy, Sentry-style error tracking on server and browser, and 200+ test files across the estate. That's what "kept alive" means.

03 — How a project runs

No surprises.
That's the whole process.

Fixed price where the scope is clear, a short paid discovery where it isn't. You see a mock-up before I build, a staging site before it goes live, and documentation when it's done.

  1. 1

    Discover

    A call, then a one-page brief with a fixed price and timeline. If I think you don't need what you asked for, I'll say so.

  2. 2

    Design

    A mock-up of the real thing — homepage, dashboard, workflow — before any code. You react to pictures, not promises.

  3. 3

    Build

    Staging first, before/after on every change, tests on anything that touches money or data. Nothing goes live until you've clicked through it.

  4. 4

    Keep it alive

    Deploys, monitoring, backups, docs. A retainer if you want one, a clean hand-off if you don't. Either way it keeps working after I leave.

04 — About

Hi, I'm Jude.

I'm a software engineer in Lagos who has spent the last six years building things for businesses in the US, the UK and at home — first as a contractor across Python, Go, TypeScript and C#, then as the one person responsible for a whole company's software.

That last part shaped how I work. When you're the only engineer, you can't hand a problem to "the backend team". You learn to design the screen, build the API, tune the query, write the deploy script and answer the 7am message when something breaks. I like that. It's also why my clients tend to stay.

I also wrote itemwise, a small open-source library, and teach Python to a private cohort on the side, which keeps me honest about explaining things clearly.
